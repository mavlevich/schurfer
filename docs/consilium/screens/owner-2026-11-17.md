## I43: Spot-price / spot-flow lead into an executable Bybit USDT perpetual

### Scope boundary (two distinct mechanisms and excluded two-leg carry)

- **Two-leg spot–perp convergence arbitrage (excluded here):** opening spot against perp
  for basis convergence belongs to `I7`/`I19` carry (already `read_fail`, four-side
  costs and borrow/margin constraints), not to `I43`.
- **I43 Mechanism 1 (primary subject of this screen — directional perp lag after a spot
  impulse):** spot price/flow moves first; we take a **one-sided directional long on the
  Bybit USDT linear perpetual** after realistic detection and execution latency, and
  measure the **subsequent net return of the perp trade** (`entry -> exit`), NOT the
  spot–perp price gap itself. A spot–perp divergence only helps if the perp moves up
  after our entry; if spot reverts down to the perp, the directional perp long loses.
- **I43 Mechanism 2 (`parked` — spot-flow continuation):** both spot and perp have
  already reacted, and continuing spot taker flow predicts further upside over tens of
  minutes. Parked immediately as a current budget decision (not claimed to be refuted):
  reopen only if a specific informational difference in spot flow is stated that was
  absent from the closed `abnormal-flow v1` and `HYP-036` (`I9`) perp-flow tests.

### 1. Mechanism (and why someone loses)

- **Confirmed fact:** Short-horizon order-flow and burst tests on Bybit linear
  perpetuals failed after costs (`abnormal-flow v1`: 251/252 episodes, mean net `-0.06%`,
  median `-0.36%`; `bybit-burst-decay-v1`: at 2.7 s delay, mean gross `+106 bps`,
  median `+33 bps` vs `41 bps` middle cost scenario, and by 46 s median `-21 bps`;
  `HYP-012b/c` cross-venue perp lead: negative standalone net on discovery and `-0.455%`
  on holdout).
- **Assumption (unverified):** On a subset of tokens/episodes, unleveraged spot demand
  initiates an impulse while perp liquidity lags long enough for a server-side Bybit
  perp taker order to capture the catch-up move from passive perp sellers or slower
  re-hedgers.
- **Unknown:** Whether any measurable fraction of spot impulses produces a repeatable
  post-delay directional perp drift rather than simultaneous repricing or spot mean
  reversion.

### 2. Sixth mandatory question: under what conditions is spot expected to lead the perpetual, and why could that lead survive until our entry?

- **Assumption / structural cases:** Normally spot and perpetual prices are tightly
  linked by cross-market arbitrageurs. Spot leading the perpetual long enough to survive
  server-side latency is plausible only under friction, such as:
  1. a large spot-only buyer executing in a thin spot book before cross-market market
     makers re-quote or hedge on the perpetual (e.g. momentary inventory/risk-limit
     saturation on the perp); or
  2. an isolated external spot market with transfer/fiat friction (overlapping with `I18`
     Upbit KRW, which remains `unscreened`).
- **Unknown:** On liquid same-venue (`Bybit spot -> Bybit perp`) or global pairs
  (`Binance spot -> Bybit perp`), how often spot moves first vs simultaneously vs after
  the perpetual, and whether any lead survives end-to-end latency.

### 3. Size and concrete cost hurdle

- **Concrete execution case (from [`screens/a-2026-11-15.md`](a-2026-11-15.md)):**
  Bybit USDT linear perpetual, non-VIP **taker in and taker out**, trade notional
  **USD 50–250**:
  - Round-trip taker fees (confirmed from Bybit fee schedule): **11.0 bps** (`5.5 bps`
    per side).
  - Program baseline non-fee spread/impact/slippage assumption: **19.0 bps** -> total
    concrete round-trip cost hurdle = **30.0 bps** (plus funding if held across a
    settlement boundary).
- **Unknown:**
  1. Actual post-impulse spread and depth impact on the Bybit perpetual specifically
     following a spot impulse (may exceed 19 bps; Bybit bar `last_bid_price`/`last_ask_price`
     lacks quote timestamps).
  2. Expected gross directional perp return after realistic end-to-end latency:
     **`unknown`** (never measured in Schurfer).
- **Hurdle check:** For the trade to clear the concrete **30.0 bps** hurdle with margin
  for post-impulse slippage and statistical uncertainty, the subsequent perp move after
  available entry must deliver substantially more than 30 bps gross on average/median,
  and converge upward in our direction (perp catching up to spot).

### 4. Count and statistical power (MDE)

- **Confirmed fact (analogous dispersion):** Alt-perp short-horizon impulse returns
  exhibit extreme dispersion (`bybit-burst-decay-v1` quartiles `-448..+472 bps`, with
  42% of paired loss in 5 instruments).
- **Unknown:** Annual count of independent spot-leading episodes (`spot first` with
  executable Bybit perp and sufficient size), asset/week clustering, and exact variance.
- **Required count / MDE estimate:** Under typical alt-perp dispersion, reaching an MDE
  around `<= 30–40 bps` requires roughly **150–300+ independent asset-clustered
  episodes** across at least **4–8+ UTC weeks** (or proportionally fewer only if the
  measured net effect is several multiples of 30 bps, per `R3`).

### 5. Ours (accessibility, latency, and venue fit)

- **Confirmed fact:** Bybit USDT perpetuals are accessible (`R4`), and our existing
  `momentumcapture` bar pipeline measures Bybit bar availability at **2.7 s median /
  7.7 s p90** after minute close (`edge-loss-decomposition-v1.md`), before signal
  computation or order routing. Even a direct trade-stream reader incurs signal-window
  accumulation + WebSocket transport + compute + REST/WS order submission latency.
- **Assumption / risk:** End-to-end retail server latency is on the order of
  **seconds**, not milliseconds. Exchange trade timestamps in historical files do NOT
  equal the timestamp at which our server could observe both feeds and fill on Bybit.

### 6. Data, minimal check, budget, and stop condition

- **What is unknown:**
  1. Lead-lag direction share (`spot first` vs `simultaneous` vs `perp first`) on public
     spot + perp trade archives;
  2. Subsequent Bybit perp net return (against the concrete **30.0 bps** taker hurdle)
     at realistic end-to-end latencies (e.g. `t + 2 s`, `t + 5 s`, `t + 10 s` after
     exchange trigger time).
- **Minimal measurement that resolves it:**
  One bounded, read-only script over **1–2 weeks of public archive `trades`** strictly
  inside the `discovery` window (`2024-10-01..2025-09-30`, zero touch of the
  `2023-02-01..2024-09-15` backward holdout or `>= 2026-09-29` blind window) on
  Bybit spot vs Bybit linear perp (and/or Binance spot vs Bybit perp), under a short
  pre-registered scope before any fetch.
- **Estimated cost / time:** ~1 day of offline script/fetch work on a bounded date
  slice; zero new live collectors, zero production changes, queued **after** `I25` and
  Stage 2A unless the owner explicitly prioritizes it.
- **Hard stop condition (`paper_fail` / `read_fail`):** Stop immediately without full
  archive downloads or collector work if either (a) `spot first` accounts for a
  negligible share (perp leads or moves simultaneously), or (b) by `t + 2..5 s` the
  subsequent perp move fails to clear the concrete **30.0 bps** Bybit taker round-trip
  hurdle.

---

**Decision (I43):** `needs_measurement` — blocked from any strategy or collector budget until a bounded 1–2 week discovery-window lead-lag and post-delay perp net check (queued behind `I25` and Stage 2A) tests whether spot actually leads Bybit perp past 30.0 bps round-trip taker costs (Mechanism 2 is `parked`).
