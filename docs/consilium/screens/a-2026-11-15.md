Read at: `27551c6` (main), consilium `bd3fba8`, PR #600 `a18f2c0`.

Status: **paper screens. No price, return, spread, book or funding value was read, on
any window.** Inputs are the repository's published notes and venue documentation read
on 2026-11-15. Account-level fees, regional schedules and archive completeness were not

## Shared cost sheet

Fees per side, base tier, from the venues' own pages:

| Venue and product                         | Taker | Maker | Source                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----: | ----: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bybit USDT perpetual, non-VIP             |   5.5 |   2.0 | [Bybit fees](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Perpetual-Futures-Contract-Fees-Explained)            |
| Bybit Innovation Zone perpetual, non-VIP  |  11.0 |   4.0 | [Bybit Innovation Zone FAQ](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/FAQ-Perpetual-Trading-Innovation-Zone) |
| Hyperliquid perpetual, tier 0, no staking |   4.5 |   1.5 | [Hyperliquid fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees)                                |

All in bps. Hyperliquid's next tier starts above USD 5M of 14-day volume, out of reach
at our size. Its staking discount is 5% of the fee for more than 10 HYPE staked and 10%
for more than 100.

Round trips, fees only:

| Structure                                 | Fees, bps |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------: |
| Bybit, taker in and out                   |      11.0 |
| Hyperliquid, taker in and out             |       9.0 |
| Bybit Innovation Zone, taker in and out   |      22.0 |
| Bybit, maker in and out                   |       4.0 |
| Hyperliquid, maker in and out             |       3.0 |
| Two legs, taker in and out on both venues |      20.0 |

The program models a Bybit taker round trip at 30 bps. Fees are 11 of that; the other
19 stand for spread, impact and slippage. The only book measurements in the repository
are Bybit's, at USD 50
([cost and power planning](../../research/cost-power-planning-v1.md)). **Nothing in the
repository measures spread or impact on Hyperliquid.** That is the largest unknown of
the first three screens.

Funding differs in schedule and not only in level. Hyperliquid pays hourly, each
payment one eighth of an 8-hour rate, on the oracle price, with an interest component
of 0.01% per 8 hours
([Hyperliquid funding](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding)).
Rates of the two venues must be put on one time base before they are compared.

History available for a later measurement: Hyperliquid publishes L2 book snapshots by
date, hour and coin, asset contexts by date, and fills, in requester-pays S3 buckets;
candles are not archived, uploads are about monthly and "data may be missing"
([Hyperliquid historical data](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/historical-data)).
The perpetual metadata has no listing date
([Hyperliquid perpetuals info](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint/perpetuals)).

## I21: move execution to Hyperliquid for the fee difference

- **Mechanism.** None; it is a cost lever.
- **Hurdle.** The fee saving is 9.0 against 11.0 bps: **2 bps per taker round trip**, 1
  bp for maker. The claim made in the round, a saving of 12 to 20 bps, is not supported
  by the fee pages. Fees are 11 of the 30 bps wall; the lever leaves the other 19
  untouched and may raise them.
- **Unknowns.** Spread and impact on Hyperliquid for the same coins at our sizes;
  whether the same asset is listed on both; collateral transfer cost and time.
- **Verdict: `paper_fail` as a fee lever.** The saving is a fifteenth of the cost wall,
  and no line on record lost by 2 bps.
- **What survives.** The cost sheet above, as the shared input of every later screen,
  and one question that is not about fees: is the non-fee cost on Hyperliquid lower than
  on Bybit for any group of coins. That needs a measurement, below.
- **Stop condition.** No further work on I21 as an idea of its own.

## I14 / I15: Bybit against Hyperliquid, two legs, around settlement

One family for the attempt budget, as C asks.

- **Mechanism claimed.** Funding and basis differ between venues and converge; traders
  with leverage on one venue only pay for it.
- **What is on record.** One exploratory screen with no protocol
  ([program plan](../../research/program-plan-2026-11.md), section 5): the funding gap
  of +44.6 bps was mostly gone within 4 hours (+11.8); the best regime, chosen after
  seeing the results, made +51.2 gross, of which +28.5 was basis; the first-minute
  figure is the Bybit leg alone. C: this is not a confirmation. A agrees.
- **Hurdle.** Four taker fills cost 20.0 bps in fees. Each leg also crosses its spread
  twice. With `x` the non-fee round-trip cost of one leg, break-even gross is
  `20 + x_bybit + x_hl`. If Hyperliquid cost what Bybit is modelled to cost (19), the
  hurdle is **58 bps**, above the best figure ever seen (+51.2, selected). The trade
  clears only if the two non-fee costs together are under about 31 bps, and only if the
  gross figure survives a protocol.
- **Unknowns.** `x_hl`; whether `x_bybit` is lower than 19 on the coins both venues
  list; the gross effect under a rule fixed in advance; the failure of one leg; margin
  on both venues; funding of both legs over the hold. Regime A had 97 events in the
  screened year, about eight a month.
- **Capital.** At USD 1,000 a leg and the selected +51.2 bps, one trade grosses about
  USD 5 before roughly USD 6 of modelled cost. Capital is a precondition for the two
  legs; it does not change the sign.
- **Verdict: `needs_measurement`, not a pass.** On paper the trade is at or below
  break-even at taker cost. A earlier called it the best candidate for the next screen;
  after the screen it is not the best candidate for the next spend.
- **Smallest next measurement.** One contract, cost only: the executable round-trip cost
  at USD 250 and USD 1,000 on both venues for the coins both list, on dates before
  2026-09-29, from Bybit's book archive and Hyperliquid's L2 archive. No return is read.
  It decides I14/I15 and the surviving question of I21 together.
- **Stop condition.** If the two non-fee costs together exceed 31 bps on the coins where
  the funding gap occurs, the family stops without a return read.

## I22: a new Hyperliquid perp against other venues in its first hours

- **Mechanism claimed.** Early traders on a fresh perp before cross-venue market makers
  are fully in.
- **Hurdle.** The same two-leg sheet: 20.0 bps of fees plus both spreads. The claimed
  mispricing exists exactly when the new perp's book is thin, so its spread is widest
  when the trade is supposed to be best. The hedge leg needs a perp on Bybit for the same
  asset that is already live; where it is live and liquid, the arbitrage is open to
  everyone.
- **Unknowns.** How many such launches a year; identity of the asset across venues and
  contract multipliers; the true first trading time on Hyperliquid, which the metadata
  does not give; the effect, `unknown`.
- **Verdict: `needs_count`.** Not a pass and not a fail: without a count of launches
  with a live Bybit perp it cannot be sized.
- **Smallest next measurement.** A count only. First-seen date per coin from the listing
  of Hyperliquid's L2 archive paths (object names, no content), joined to Bybit launch
  times from the instrument catalog that the catalyst fetch already keeps. Under C's
  rule, an incomplete history is `unknown`, not a negative.
- **Stop condition.** Fewer than 40 launches with a Bybit perp live at least 24 hours
  earlier, over the windows we may use: stop.

## I25: short the drift of a new Bybit perp from several days after listing

- **Mechanism claimed.** Late buyers, and holders of free or unlocked tokens who sell.
- **Why it is not obviously ours.** The mechanism is widely known, and the market has a
  price for it: funding. If shorts pay to hold, that payment is the crowd's estimate of
  the drift. The idea has an edge only where the drift exceeds the funding paid, which is
  a narrower claim than "new listings fall".
- **Hurdle.** One leg. New perps often sit in the Innovation Zone, where the non-VIP
  taker fee is 11 bps a side: 22 bps of fees a round trip, not 11. Spread on a
  days-old perp is `unknown` and likely above the 19 bps modelled for established ones. Funding
  over a multi-day short is a cost or an income of unknown sign and can exceed both.
  A working hurdle before any measurement: **above 41 bps plus funding paid**.
- **Count.** Available without a new request: the catalyst fetch keeps `launch_ms` for
  every USDT linear perpetual, closed ones included, launched before 2025-10-01. The
  number of launches per window falls out of that fetch. Until then it is `unknown`; "a
  few a week" is A's recollection, not a measurement.
- **Dependence.** New perps move with the alt market, and listings cluster in hot
  months. Over a year in which alts fell, a short of anything profits. The test is the
  market-adjusted net of the program plan's rule 2, on launch clusters by week, with
  delisted instruments included. Distinct tickers launched in one week are one episode
  for power.
- **Verdict: `needs_count`, the strongest of the four on paper.** It is the only one
  with one leg, a horizon long enough to dwarf the fee, and a count that may be large.
  None of that is evidence of a return.
- **Smallest next measurement.** The launch count per ISO week and window from the
  catalyst fetch's catalog, with no price. If it supports a test, a short fixed protocol
  before any price is read: one entry age, one horizon, funding included, market
  adjusted, at most three variants.
- **Stop condition.** Fewer than about 100 launches in the discovery year, or fewer than
  20 launch weeks: stop before a protocol.
